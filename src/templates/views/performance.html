CPU Governor

The rule the CPU follows when it picks a clock speed. Powersave stays low until work actually arrives — cooler, quieter, longer battery. Performance holds the clock high, so the machine answers instantly and runs hotter and drains sooner because of it. This is separate from the clock limits below: the governor chooses inside those limits, it cannot go past them.

Power Profile

One switch that tunes CPU, graphics and platform power together, and it may move the CPU governor with it. The clock ceilings on the next card are kept per power source, not per profile, so switching profile here does not move them.

Clock Limits

The fastest and the slowest the CPU may run, saved separately for each power source. These are TLP's own settings — CPU_SCALING_MAX_FREQ_ON_AC and its neighbours — and the same values the Power view edits. TLP applies them itself on every boot and again whenever the machine switches between mains and battery, so a limit set here holds without this app running. Lowering the CPU ceiling is the main lever on heat and fan noise: the machine stays quiet and lasts longer on battery, and you give up the short bursts that make a heavy page or a big build feel instant. Nothing here reaches TLP until you press Apply now.

Turbo Boost

Lets the CPU push past its base clock in short bursts. Switched off, the peak speed drops and the heat and fan noise drop noticeably with it — worth trying on your lap or in a quiet room. The clock ceiling above is the harder limit: turbo can only reach as far as that ceiling allows.
